Research papers

Insects

  1. Chen 2026 which does a lot of what we want with the same species but is generally bad quality and probably not very reliable in the details (e.g. they discuss the Cmac Y identified by Kaufmann 2023, but reference the wrong contig name)
  2. DTOL comparative paper
  3. Mahajan 2017 (Diptera):
    • They use a subtraction pipeline to identify male-specific transcripts, where they remove RNA reads that map to females, assemble a transcriptome from the putatively male-specific ones, do some more filtering, and obtain relatively reliable Y-linked transcripts.
    • Y-genes come from wherever, no enrichment of non-Y homologs on the X
    • Identify gene age with Ka vs. Ks, there are genes of all ages, probably gradual acquisition
    • in young Y chromosomes, the most diverged genes have testes-biased expression
    • Also interesting discussion on method limitations caused by assembly quality
  4. Yu 2026: super conserved noncoding sex determining locus in (haplodiploid) hymenoptera
  5. Vicoso & Bachtrog 2015: Sex chromsome turnover in Diptera
  6. Krsticevi 2015: resolving tandem duplicates on Drosophila Y with single molecule sequencing
  7. Bernardo Carvalho 2015: birth of FDY on Drosophila Y through segmental duplication followed by translocation, were the four other affected genes became pseudogenized but not this one.
  8. Hall 2016Fast Y evolution in mosquitoes after radiation (species radiation not nuclear)

Reviews

  1. Ellegren 2011 on the influence of heterogameity on sex chromosome evolution:
    • "For example, long interspersed repeat elements are enriched on both the mammalian X and the avian Z chromosome [46,47], whereas gene density is lower than on autosomes in both systems as a result of intergenic expansions [27,48]"
    • He also says about the selection pressure on X-linked genes in the heterogametic sex that "selection will occur more frequently" as opposed to that it is stronger, which I think doesn't change the outcome because the selection is stronger in the end compared to the autosomes due to it occuring more frequently
    • "Among the genes that generate new retrocopies, through mRNA intermediates, there is an excess of X-linked genes inserted at autosomal locations." Might be because X linked genes are temporarily inactivated during meiosis in males (meiotic sex-chromosome inactivation, MSCI), and genes that give selective advantage to males want to escape. The retrocopies that leave often aquire male-specific function, because, if dominant, they spread easier on the autosomes because they are temporarily inactivated on the X negating their selective advantage
